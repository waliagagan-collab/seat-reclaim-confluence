Checked and sound:

- Every resolver goes through the admin check, asked of Confluence as the calling user; any failure refuses. The three Rovo actions run the same check. The queue worker cannot be called by a user. A test enforces the first.
- Licence: `startScan` and `reclaim` are refused in production without an active licence; the list, History, export and Undo stay open on purpose, so a site whose subscription lapsed can still give access back. The message tells the admin where to subscribe.
- Scopes: the eight scopes in the manifest are the eight the live listing shows. Each has a call that uses it. One write scope, used for two calls, both as the signed-in admin. No egress.
- The 10 Nov fixes, read again in today's code: History paging keeps every entry; a reclaim acts only on people on the current list; a paused scan resumes from the status poll; whiteboards and databases count as active; report rows keep only the groups that grant Confluence. All hold.
- Forge limits: the stored values stay under 240 KiB for the site sizes in the price table; the response of the largest call is about 1.5 MB by my estimate, against a 5 MB limit; reclaim runs three people per call and writes the History entry before the first change.
- Nothing secret in the repo. No account names, emails or tokens in code or tests beyond made-up ones.

## 6. What I chose not to change, and why

- **A per-person admin check (A1).** The idea: just before a reclaim, read the person from the user search with their operations and skip anyone who may administer Confluence, as the Jira app asks Jira. I did not build it because I could not see, without a browser, whether the operations in that answer belong to the person looked up or to the admin asking. If they belong to the admin asking, every reclaim would be refused. Check 6 above settles it; after that it is about fifteen lines and no new scope.
- **The name rule on every group (10 Nov owner decision 1).** Leaving alone anyone in any group with "admin" in its name, as the Jira app does, is still the owner's call. It would lower the "Ready" count on sites where Jira admins hold unused Confluence seats, which is the case the app exists for.
- **Personal data reporting (A2).** It needs a new scope, so a major version that every customer has to approve. Proposed, not done. If it is added, Permissions & Access Review for Confluence already has the pattern (`confluence-permissions/manifest.yml`): scope `report:personal-data`, a weekly scheduled job that reports the stored account IDs, and removal of the History rows, list rows and protected entries of closed accounts.
- **The 8 moderate `npm audit` findings.** All are `glob` and `minimatch` inside `@forge/manifest`, which `@forge/api` and `@forge/bridge` bring in. The fixed versions are a major release beyond what those packages accept, so a targeted `npm update` cannot reach them. They are not in code that runs for a customer. No high or critical findings.
- **A9 and A10.** Neither can be reached on the site sizes seen so far, and each fix would touch the storage layout or the list loading for little gain.
- **The banners and the old captures in `marketplace/assets/`.** Not on the listing. Rebuilding the banners needs the new icon and an image library this machine does not have. Left, with a note in the script; they are named in section 1 so nobody uploads them.
- **Other wording on the page** (the dashes in the progress lines, the long sentence in the confirm dialog). Every wording change makes a screenshot old. I changed only what was wrong or hard to read.
- **The warning for people "counted as active"** does not mention the new 404 case. It is rare, and the sentence is already long.
- **The website and the Marketplace.** Not mine to touch. The sentences are in section 1 and in the summary.
- **`listing.md` above the new section.** Left as it was, as the brief asks, although its Name section and one sentence of More details are out of date.

One note on the run: a combined command (build, development deploy, then a filter on the install list that contained the word "production") was refused by the session's command rules. I did not try to get round it. I ran the development deploy on its own, in the form that had been allowed earlier in the run; it failed once on a network error and went through on the second try. No production command was run at any point.
